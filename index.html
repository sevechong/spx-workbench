Geopolitics

Iran conflict reignites, oil spikes

Stocks were falling and oil prices were rising amid renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities in the Strait of Hormuz, after two oil tankers, one Saudi and one South Korean-owned, were hit by projectiles as the U.S. and Iran resumed hostilities in a six-month war that appears to be locked in a stalemate.

President Trump threatened further strikes against Iran after Tehran launched missiles at two U.S. vessels, according to Benzinga, keeping the conflict in headlines through Tuesday's session.

Strikes between the US and Iran in the Persian Gulf prolonged the suspension of energy exports from the region and added to pro-inflationary risks on the global economy, consolidating the likelihood that major central banks are due to restart their hiking cycles this year.

The Fed & Rates

Hike odds surge on hot inflation talk

On Aug 28, at the Fed's Jackson Hole symposium, Chairman Kevin Warsh warned about sticky inflation, after which CME's FedWatch tool showed a 57.5% probability of a 25 basis-point hike to 3.75-4% in September.

By Tuesday morning, Schwab reported that chances of a rate hike at the Fed's September meeting reached 66% today, according to the CME FedWatch Tool, up from 40% a week ago.

Treasury yields are now a stone's throw from 5% for the 10-year note, and the 30-year yield has spent 55 days above 5% so far this year, the most in any year since 2006.

AI & Tech

Data-center boom meets a rough stock year

Barclays' Stefano Pascale said "Big Tech (Mag7 ex-Tesla) has lagged the S & P through the vast majority of 2026," adding that the group has underperformed the S&P 500 for 92% of the year to date, putting it on pace for one of its worst 12-month periods since 2013, second only to 2022.

Even so, capital spending on AI infrastructure keeps rising: the Magnificent Seven alone are expected to deploy $527 billion in AI/data center capital expenditures in fiscal 2026, up $62 billion from prior estimates.

Traders are also weighing an intensifying domestic debate over AI data centers, per Benzinga, alongside a leadership change at Apple, where shares rose this morning as John Ternus succeeds Tim Cook as CEO.

Energy leads YTD, tech lags today

Eight of the 11 S&P sectors are higher so far in 2026, with energy leading, up 43%, while Consumer Discretionary is lagging, down 2.3%.

Within Tuesday's session, communication and consumer defensive stocks are leading sector gainers, while technology and industrials are underperforming.

Breadth was weak: the S&P 500 had 12 companies with 52-week lows and seven with 52-week highs on Tuesday, and Nike valued at $38.07, a level the athletic retailer hasn't traded at in over twenty years, while travel and casino names including Wynn Resorts, Las Vegas Sands, VICI Properties and Carnival hit new lows, even as healthcare names like Pfizer and energy names notched new highs.
