Geopolitics

Iran, Strait of Hormuz, oil

Crude and Treasuries whipsawed over the weekend after Bloomberg reported that bonds and stocks dropped as Middle East tensions flared following President Donald Trump's rejection of Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, reviving a conflict that has driven much of this year's energy rally.

U.S. crude traded near $94 a barrel on Monday, with Crude Oil rising to 94.10 USD/Bbl on September 28, 2026, up 1.83% from the previous day, according to Trading Economics, while diesel prices stay far above pre-war levels.

Away from the Gulf, Charles Schwab's market update noted that meetings between U.S. and Chinese leaders failed to forge any trade breakthroughs, leaving chip export policy toward China unresolved heading into this week.

The Fed and rates

Hiking, not cutting

The Fed has already tightened once this month: on September 16, the FOMC unanimously raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%, and 16 of the 18 officials submitting projections expected at least one additional increase before the end of 2026, per FinanceFeeds.

New York Fed President John Williams told a London conference on September 24 that "another rate hike may be appropriate by the end of the year," though he did not say the move should come at the October 27-28 meeting; Polymarket currently shows 65% odds of a 25bp hike versus 34% for no change at that meeting.

The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed sharply this month, as it dipped to 4.963% on Monday, then closed higher on each of the next four sessions: 4.968%, 5.114%, 5.162% and 5.184%, a move that has also pushed the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate to 7.37%, its highest in years.

AI and tech

Capex, chips, and politics

Business spending on AI infrastructure looks resilient: orders for non-defense capital goods excluding aircraft, a closely watched proxy for business investment, rose 1.6% in August after an upwardly revised 0.6% increase in July, beating economist forecasts and reflecting continued AI-linked capital spending.

Chip export policy stayed contentious after last week's summit: Senate Democrats used Xi Jinping's state visit to press Republicans for votes on export control legislation restricting Beijing's access to advanced AI chips, while The Motley Fool noted Nvidia's $108.0 billion revenue forecast for its fiscal third quarter assumes no data center compute revenue from China.

Supply constraints and concentration remain watch items: JPMorgan estimates that approximately 60% of data center capacity planned for completion in 2027 hasn't even broken ground yet, while Morgan Stanley flags that the 10 largest stocks in the index account for about 40% of its total value.

Market internals

Narrow leadership, big caps

Friday's session showed concentrated leadership: Technology and Industrials sat above the rest of the table, while Utilities sat third from the bottom and Real Estate was down 0.41%, so the advance was concentrated rather than broad, per Benzinga.

Among large caps, Trading Economics reported Dow gains were led by Microsoft (3.71%), Sherwin-Williams (2.72%) and Amgen (2.15%), while the biggest losers were Salesforce (-1.76%), IBM (-0.71%) and Nike (-0.61%), and Meta shares stayed elevated after its AI agent launch.

Small caps kept lagging the broader tape: for the week ending Friday, the S&P 500 rose 1.21%, the Nasdaq gained 2.06%, and the Russell 2000 fell 0.80%, underscoring how much of the rally sits in a handful of large names.
