Geopolitics

Iran, China summit, trade truce

Equity futures were little changed early Wednesday as oil prices fluctuated amid fresh uncertainty around the war between the U.S. and Iran, now well into its sixth month according to reporting cited by UBS.

Traders are also focused on Xi Jinping's Washington visit; his summit with Trump is scheduled for Thursday, and analysts at the Council on Foreign Relations say the meeting isn't likely to produce many deliverables, with a temporary extension of the trade truce seen as the most likely outcome.

That truce carries its own clock: reporting notes a 90-day tariff truce expires around November 10, and both governments have described extending it as the floor outcome each delegation needs from the talks.

The Fed and rates

First hike since 2023

On September 16 the Fed, under new Chair Kevin Warsh, raised the target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase since 2023, and Warsh said the move was aimed at restoring the central bank's inflation-fighting credibility.

The 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched 5% after the decision before easing back to roughly 4.93% this week, even as regional Fed presidents Goolsbee and Musalem argued further increases may still be needed.

Futures-market pricing tracked by Polymarket currently shows the October 27–28 FOMC meeting essentially split, with no-change and a further 25bp hike each carrying roughly even odds.

AI and tech

Chips lead, politics enters

A rally in chipmakers drove the Nasdaq 100 to its first record since June, with Asian bellwethers Samsung and SK Hynix also climbing on the move.

Nvidia has guided third-quarter revenue to roughly $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, with gross margins around 74%, as its next-generation Vera Rubin platform begins ramping for what CEO Jensen Huang calls the era of agentic AI.

AI has also become a diplomatic line item: CSIS notes the Trump-Xi agenda is expected to touch AI safeguards and a possible AI safety notification mechanism alongside tariffs and Taiwan.
