Geopolitics

Iran conflict keeps oil pinned near $100

Oil prices have advanced more than 18% in September as the market braces for a prolonged war in the Middle East, according to CNBC. Futures were pointing lower Tuesday as energy prices continue to surge amid the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict, with Brent crude reaching $107.48 a barrel, Benzinga reported.

Yahoo Finance noted that prices have remained elevated since Saudi Arabia shuttered its East-West pipeline and the Iranian-backed Houthis launched fresh attacks in the Middle East. Trading Economics added that oil prices resumed their climb as Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline remained shut, while Ukraine disputed President Trump's claim that it had already reached an agreement with Russia to halt attacks on energy infrastructure.

The energy-rates linkage matters for stocks too: CNBC cited a strategist noting the one-month rolling correlation between front-month West Texas Intermediate crude and the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to 0.96, and "higher oil prices lead to higher inflation expectations and vice versa."

The Fed and rates

A hike, not a cut, is now priced in

The September 2026 FOMC meeting will take place on 15-16 September, with the rate decision and policy statement due on 16 September, per ATFX. Trading Economics reported markets are pricing in roughly a 92% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike by the Federal Reserve on Wednesday, which would mark the first increase since July 2023.

Bonds are moving hard ahead of the decision. CNBC reported the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to its highest level since 2007 on Tuesday as a sell-off in U.S. government debt deepened ahead of the Federal Reserve's interest-rate decision, and the 30-year Treasury bond, more sensitive to geopolitical risks, rose 7 basis points to 5.4%.

Yahoo Finance noted the Federal Open Market Committee is scheduled to begin its September meeting on Tuesday, and traders overwhelmingly expect a rate hike, with the dot-plot and Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's press conference expected to offer clues on the path ahead.

AI and tech

A safety essay rattled chip stocks

Forbes reported that in a roughly 3,900-word essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier," the Anthropic co-founder and CEO wrote that "we must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." Within a day, rivals responded: Forbes noted OpenAI CEO Sam Altman posted on X that "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier," and Elon Musk's reaction ran three words: "Dario is right."

The market reaction was swift. Bloomberg reported the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index tumbled 5.9%, with Nvidia Corp. and Intel Corp. dropping 3.4% and 5.6%, respectively. CNBC separately reported that Anthropic has picked the Nasdaq as the exchange for its potential IPO, even as its own CEO argues for slowing down.

There's a political angle too. Tech Insider observed that because the call came from a sitting frontier-lab CEO rather than outside critics, it changes the credibility calculus regulators in Washington and Brussels will apply to it.
