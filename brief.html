Geopolitics: Iran war meets a superpower summit

Two fronts, one oil market

The Iran war that began in late February has repeatedly disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint through which over 20% of the world's oil trade passes, keeping crude prices structurally elevated all year.

Stocks rallied Monday partly because Asian stocks were poised to climb after a tech-fueled rally on Wall Street and a drop in oil prices amid hopes for diplomatic progress in the Iran war, though no ceasefire has been confirmed.

Separately, the summit between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping is expected to cover trade, investment, AI, the war with Iran, and Taiwan, with the meeting set for Thursday at the White House.

Tech leadership is literally in the room: technology executives, including Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Alphabet's Sundar Pichai, OpenAI's Sam Altman, Apple's Tim Cook, Tesla's Elon Musk and Nvidia's Jensen Huang, are expected to attend the dinner, and CNBC noted Trump, facing low approval on the economy ahead of the midterm election, has an added incentive to emerge from the summit with China's Xi touting trade wins.

The Fed and rates: hiking, not cutting

A new, more hawkish regime

Under new chair Kevin Warsh, Fed policymakers unanimously voted to raise rates 25 basis points, the first hike since 2023, and vowed to fight inflation, taking the target range to 3.75%–4% as the Fed's target range is now 3.75% to 4%, up from 3.5% to 3.75% where it had been since a cut last December.

The Fed's own projections point to more tightening ahead, since 16 of 19 members of the Federal Open Market Committee expect at least one more rate hike this year, according to the Fed's "dot plot" of projections, and Trading Economics reported the benchmark yield surged to a 19-year high as the Fed raised interest rates and signaled at least one more hike this year.

Fed officials are still talking tough: Chicago's Goolsbee said he remains open to the possibility that inflation will resume its decline towards the 2% target, but warned that interest rates may need to rise if that fails to materialise, while Minneapolis's Kashkari said inflation remains too high and that price pressures have expanded beyond the oil-price shock linked to the Iran war. Prediction-market pricing for the Oct 28 decision currently shows 55% 25 bps hike, 45% No change.

AI and tech: chips lead, financing gets scrutinized

Compute demand meets power limits

Monday's rally was driven by AI enthusiasm: chipmakers surged as early signs of success for Meta Platforms Inc.'s AI agent sparked enthusiasm about the industry, and the social-media firm jumped 11%, while separately Advanced Micro Devices Inc. topped $1 trillion in market value.

The fundamentals still look strong on paper: Broadcom said AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion, up 221% year on year and 54% sequentially, and now represents 56% of total revenue, up from 49% in Q2, and guided Q4 AI semiconductor revenue to $21.7 billion, up 236% year over year.

The political dimension is explicit ahead of the summit, since the Trump administration has pushed back against the need for additional regulations or any US slowdown in developing artificial intelligence, saying, among other things, that this would hand China the advantage. Meanwhile financing and siting friction is building: trackers cited by one AI-infrastructure newsletter found at least 48 projects worth about $156 billion that have been blocked or stalled by local opposition, and Oregon's governor froze all data center requests on state land through July 1, 2027.

Market internals: narrow leadership under the record

Mega-caps carry a thin market

Monday's advance was led by growth stocks: the Nasdaq Composite led the advance, gaining 2.26%, while the S&P 500 rose 1.49% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 0.71%, with the move helped as chipmakers rose as the AI trade appeared to regain its footing.

But sentiment and breadth tell a more cautious story: Schwab noted respondents who called themselves "bearish" rose to 53.3%, the highest since May 2025, which could help explain the recent sharp decline in market breadth, as last week ended with only around 30% of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day moving average.

Volatility has stayed unusually quiet through all of this, with one trading note observing the VIX is holding near 14.87, suggesting that investors are not currently pricing in severe near-term market volatility, even as gains concentrate in a small number of AI-linked names like the newly minted $1 trillion AMD.
