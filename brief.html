Geopolitics

Iran war, oil, and a Trump-Xi summit

The Iran conflict is now in its seventh month, and oil has been the main transmission channel into equities and bonds; shipping disruptions forced major OPEC members including Saudi Arabia to cut output to its lowest level since 1990, while the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve sits near a record low. There is a tentative bright spot: US and Iranian negotiators in New York are reportedly weighing a phased deal in which Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for the US lifting its economic blockade, though this is unconfirmed.

Separately, President Trump hosted President Xi in Washington this week for a summit covering trade, artificial intelligence, and broader strategic issues, and Xi told Trump there is more room for cooperation than competition on AI in a Chinese state-media readout of the meeting. On tariffs, the effective US tariff on Chinese goods is now estimated around 23%, well down from last year's 145% peak but still triple the level applied to other major trading partners.

The Fed and rates

First hike since 2023, more flagged

The Federal Reserve voted unanimously to raise the federal funds rate a quarter point, to a new target range of 3.75% to 4%, its first increase in over three years. Chair Kevin Warsh explained the shift by pointing to three changes since the July meeting: the economy strengthened, inflation didn't slow, and geopolitical tensions intensified.

The committee's own projections suggest this may not be the last move — a solid majority of officials, 16 of 18, expect at least one additional hike before year-end. Bond markets have reacted accordingly: at Thursday's settlement, the 10-year yield rose about 6.9 basis points to 5.179%, with the 30-year up to 5.467%, part of a broader multidecade repricing.

The next scheduled decision falls on October 28, 2026, and futures pricing currently implies a 54% market-assigned probability of another 25-basis-point hike at that meeting, though this can move quickly with new data.

AI and tech

Capex boom, chip valuations, political spotlight

The scale of AI infrastructure spending is still striking: major tech companies are estimated to spend around $650 billion on AI data centers in 2026 alone. Not everything is smooth, however — Oracle has reportedly invoked a force-majeure clause to try to avoid payments on a New Mexico data center it is building with a Blue Owl Capital unit, should the project slip.

Semiconductor results remain enormous even where stock reactions look muted: Nvidia grew revenue 106% year-over-year to $96.2 billion in its most recent fiscal quarter, while elsewhere in the sector Advanced Micro Devices' shares rallied enough this month to push its market capitalization past $1 trillion. AI also has a direct political dimension right now, since it was one of the named topics at this week's Trump-Xi summit alongside tariffs and rare earths.
