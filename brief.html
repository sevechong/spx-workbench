Geopolitics

Shutdown averted, Iran talks ongoing

A government shutdown that could have landed right at the start of October was averted after Congress enacted and President Trump signed a continuing resolution on September 2, 2026, extending appropriations through December 11 and preventing a funding lapse when fiscal year 2027 began October 1.

That bill cleared the Senate 90-6 and the House 370-48 under suspension of the rules, reflecting broad bipartisan agreement to avoid disruption ahead of the midterms, which removes a source of October noise many traders had been pricing in.

On oil, prices declined Thursday as investors weighed the outlook for US-Iran peace talks against signs that crude exports from the Gulf region are recovering. Separately, Brent fell toward $97 after major banks and trackers said Persian Gulf oil exports were close to pre-war levels, even as Hormuz tanker attacks continued, so the risk premium has not fully disappeared.

The Fed and rates

Yields are leading, not the Fed

Core PCE inflation rose just 0.2% month-over-month in August versus a 0.3% forecast, with the annual rate easing to 3.0%, a softer print that initially helped sentiment.

Yet the 10-year Treasury yield appeared to matter more than Federal Reserve hike odds this week, and that shifts the risk picture into Friday's jobs report. Odds of an October move have swung hard: October hike odds, as tracked by CME FedWatch, fell from roughly 70.9% on September 23 to about 34.9% by September 30 while the 10-year climbed.

Prediction-market pricing for the October 27-28 FOMC meeting currently shows a hold as the favorite at roughly 66%, with a quarter-point hike near 35% and a cut around 2%, underscoring that this cycle's debate is still hike-versus-hold, not hold-versus-cut.

AI and tech

Micron beats, safety debate simmers

Futures got a lift after Micron reported fourth-quarter earnings that beat Wall Street's expectations and raised its first-quarter outlook, and US equity futures pushed higher Thursday morning as Micron's outlook reignited appetite for artificial-intelligence names, with Nasdaq-100 futures surging about 1.0% and S&P 500 futures adding 0.6%.

The AI story has a political and safety dimension too: chip stocks wobbled on news that OpenAI paused training of its most capable model and halted a rollout for safety reasons, even as Nvidia's CEO publicly defended AI safety. At the same time, Nvidia's announcement of an additional $150 billion share buyback signaled chip revenue remains firm, at least for the parts Nvidia itself makes.

The underlying capex wave is large: major technology companies were estimated to spend about $650 billion on AI data centers in 2026, a figure that keeps hyperscaler earnings central to the broader index's direction.

Market internals

Narrow leadership, index masks damage

September showed a split tape: the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.8% and hit a new peak on September 22, with Meta, AMD and a handful of other semiconductor names driving that performance, while the S&P 500 declined 0.4% for the month and sat 1.9% below its record high set in August.

Under the surface, breadth has been weaker than the headline index suggests: tech led in September as rates spiked, cyclicals and defensives lagged, and there was notable selling down the cap spectrum and internationally even as mega-caps rallied. A similar pattern showed up this week, where the index barely moved on Wednesday while the average stock in it fell roughly three times as much, with options pricing less protection for that gap than usual.

On Wednesday's sector scoreboard, five sectors rose and six fell, with growth sectors holding two of the top three spots and technology sitting well clear of the next-best sector; Communication Services and Energy led, while Utilities, Financials and Industrials brought up the rear.
