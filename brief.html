The Fed and rates

A hike, not a cut, and 5% yields

The Federal Reserve raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4%, as expected, at its September meeting, and that move marked the central bank's first hike in three years. The Fed also signalled at least one more rate hike this year, with Chair Warsh reaffirming the central bank's commitment to taming inflation.

Bond markets reacted sharply: the benchmark 10-year yield topped 5.04% ahead of the FOMC decision, its highest level since 2007, amid concerns about the Fed's ability to control inflation and persistently elevated oil prices. The stock reaction was rough too, with one CNBC headline simply reading "Dow drops 600 points as Fed rate hike and Warsh's inflation talk unnerve investors."

The next FOMC meeting is October 27-28, 2026, and heading into it the confirmed starting point is the current target range of 3.75%-4%. Prediction markets currently lean toward another quarter-point hike rather than a hold or cut.
